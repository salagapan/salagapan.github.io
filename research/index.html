Research

How brain networks generate behavior and symptoms, and how stimulation can steer them.

Our work combines human intracranial and noninvasive electrophysiology, brain stimulation, and interpretable machine learning. The common thread is to measure network dynamics in people, understand what they mean, and use that understanding to design better neuromodulation therapies.

Biomarkers of recovery with deep brain stimulation for depression

Deep brain stimulation (DBS) of the subcallosal cingulate can produce lasting relief for some people with treatment-resistant depression, but clinicians have lacked an objective readout of how a patient is doing. Using chronic local field potential recordings from the DBS device, we identified cingulate dynamics that track recovery and can help tell a relapse apart from a bad day (Nature, 2023). Ongoing work extends these biomarkers to recordings collected at home, to sleep and interoceptive processing, to evoked responses to stimulation, and to explainable AI models of treatment response. We also use biophysical and neural mass models to interpret the mechanisms behind these signals.

Brain network dynamics of motivation and effort

Many psychiatric and neurological conditions disrupt motivation and the willingness to exert effort. We study how cingulate and insular circuits support effort-based decision making, using intracranial recordings (stereo-EEG) in people with epilepsy, naturalistic tasks such as cycling and incline walking, and multimodal measures of brain-body interaction. Intracranial stimulation lets us test whether these dynamics play a causal role.

Intracranial stimulation of cognition

Earlier work showed that the effect of direct cortical stimulation depends on the brain state at the time of stimulation (PLoS Biology, 2016), and that network-targeted, multi-site stimulation can enhance working memory by changing the timing relationships among low-frequency oscillations (Cell Reports, 2019). We also developed methods to remove stimulation artifacts from intracranial recordings.

NeuroAI and interpretable models

We develop machine learning methods that stay interpretable to neuroscientists and clinicians. Examples include probabilistic decomposed linear dynamical systems for discovering latent neural dynamics (NeurIPS, 2024) and methods that jointly learn the dimensionality and structure of subjective perceptual spaces from ordinal data (LORE, 2026).

Noninvasive brain stimulation

During my postdoctoral work, I contributed to randomized clinical trials of transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS) targeting brain oscillations in depression and schizophrenia, and to studies of closed-loop stimulation during sleep.
