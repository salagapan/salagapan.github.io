Sankar Alagapan

Senior Research Scientist, School of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Georgia Institute of Technology

I am a research faculty member in the School of Electrical and Computer Engineering at Georgia Tech, where I co-direct the Structured Information for Precision Neuroengineering Lab. I am an engineer by education and a neuroscientist by training. My work sits at the intersection of neurotechnology and clinical neuroscience, with the goal of developing brain stimulation therapies for psychiatric and neurological disorders.

My research focuses on human brain network dynamics and neuromodulation. We record from the human brain with invasive and noninvasive electrophysiology, including stereo-EEG, scalp EEG, and local field potentials from deep brain stimulation (DBS) devices. We then use intracranial stimulation and interpretable machine learning to understand and steer these dynamics. Current directions include:

  • Electrophysiological biomarkers for DBS in treatment-resistant depression: tracking recovery from chronic recordings and using them to guide therapy.
  • Network dynamics of motivation and effort: studying how cingulate and insular circuits support effort-based decision making, using naturalistic tasks and intracranial recordings.
  • NeuroAI and interpretable models: developing machine learning and dynamical-systems methods that make neural data and its relationship to behavior easier to interpret.

I received my PhD in Biomedical Engineering from the University of Florida. Before joining Georgia Tech, I was a postdoctoral research associate at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill in the Carolina Center for Neurostimulation, where I worked on intracranial and transcranial brain stimulation. I am a Georgia CTSA KL2 Scholar.

Outside the lab, I enjoy photography, travel (to a certain extent), and (re)learning history.
